Digital Business Solutions

A CRM, short for customer relationship manager, is just a system for keeping track of who your customers are, what they've bought or asked for, and what still needs a follow up call, so that information doesn't live only in one person's head or one stack of sticky notes.

An ERP, short for enterprise resource planner, connects the money side of the business to the physical side, inventory, orders, invoicing, and payroll, so the same numbers show up everywhere instead of getting retyped by hand into three different spreadsheets that never quite agree.

Is either one actually right for you?

A CRM is worth it if keeping track of customers depends on memory, a notebook, or whoever answered the phone that day. It's not worth it if the business doesn't really have repeat customers or ongoing relationships to track in the first place.

An ERP is worth it once inventory, orders, and invoicing live in separate spreadsheets that never quite match. It's not worth it yet if the operation is small enough that one person can keep it all straight without much trouble.

Either way, off the shelf software isn't the only option. An existing system can be pointed to when one already fits the business, and something simple and custom can be built when nothing on the market does.
